# Raw notes — Meltzer, Critical Reader 5th Ed — Front matter: study method, stem taxonomy, reading diet

Source scope: "How to Use This Book," the question-stem categorization list, and the outside-reading recommendations (pre-Chapter-1 material). Extraction is understanding-only; no source wording retained.

## Concepts taught

- `rw-stem-recognition` — A student can classify any Reading question into its skill family purely from the wording of the stem, before reading the passage. Stem phrasing is a reliable, finite signal set; recognizing it instantly tells you which reasoning procedure to run.
- `rw-diagnose-then-drill-loop` — The expert prep loop: full official adaptive test → sort every miss by stem family → drill only the weak families → retest. Error CLUSTERING decides study order; scattered errors mean study everything in sequence instead.
- `rw-strategy-recall-under-mixing` — Isolated single-skill practice systematically overstates readiness: on a real test, types arrive shuffled, so the student must also RECOGNIZE which procedure applies with no label attached. This recognition step is its own trainable skill and a distinct failure mode (knows the method, can't tell when to use it).
- `rw-untimed-mastery-first` — Deliberate two-phase training: first do full tests untimed (or in untimed question pools), consciously naming the applicable strategy before each item, until strategy selection is automatic; only then reintroduce the clock. Accuracy-then-speed, never both at once.
- `rw-error-autopsy` — No test attempt is finished until every miss has a known root cause and a stated prevention plan. Doubles as conservation: official material is scarce, so extract maximal signal per test.
- `rw-controllables-mindset` — Reading is inherently noisier than Math (an item can genuinely hinge on something unforeseeable), but the majority of misses trace to controllable process failures: rushing, vague goal, skipped steps. Score gains come from controlling the controllables, not from hoping.
- `rw-background-reading-diet` — Sustained exposure to adult-level nonfiction (science journalism, essays, quality magazines) plus literary fiction builds the register familiarity the test presumes. Passive exposure is insufficient — the reading must be active: mark claims, transitions, tone words; summarize the argument.

## The stem taxonomy (as this source carves it — 8 families)

1. Vocabulary — fill-the-blank with the most logical word, or meaning-in-context of a common word.
2. Big picture / main idea — central claim of the whole text, or its overall organization.
3. Literal comprehension — what the text states about X; why (per the text) something happens.
4. Function — rhetorical purpose of the whole passage or of one underlined piece within it.
5. Text completion — pick the statement that logically finishes the passage's argument.
6. Support / undermine — pick the quotation or finding that would strengthen or weaken a stated claim.
7. Graphic — pick the data description from a chart/table that bears on the claim.
8. Paired texts — (appears in ch1 list) predict how one author would respond to the other.

## Teaching philosophy observed

- Diagnostics precede instruction; the book is a reference to be entered through one's error profile, not read linearly (unless errors are diffuse or time is long).
- Explicit metacognition: the student should verbalize WHICH strategy an item calls for before solving, until the selection becomes silent and automatic. The author frames the endgame as self-sufficiency — no coach will be present on test day.
- Slow-is-smooth sequencing: process fidelity first, speed as a byproduct of automaticity.

## Expert reasoning patterns (generalized)

- Experts index their entire toolkit off stem language; novices index off passage topic. Teaching students to key on stems is the fastest transfer from practice to test.
- Experts treat a practice test as data (miss taxonomy) rather than as a score event.

## Misconceptions targeted (and why students hold them)

- "Doing many practice tests raises scores by itself." Held because volume feels like work and scores do drift slightly; actually plateaus without per-error autopsy. Evidence signal: repeated tests, flat score.
- "If I can do each drill type, I'm ready." Held because chapter exercises are self-labeled; the mixed-test recognition burden is invisible until the real thing. Produces the classic drills-great/test-flat gap.
- "Reading misses are random / just how I read." Held because Reading feedback is less crisply right/wrong-with-a-rule than grammar or math; actually most misses have a recurring, nameable cause.

## What this source does well / poorly here

- Well: cleanest procedure-first stem taxonomy we've seen; the "bridge" insight (recognition-under-mixing as a separate trainable skill) is a genuinely useful diagnostic category our engine could tag (drill accuracy high + test accuracy low on same skill = recognition failure, not skill failure).
- Well: frames error review as non-negotiable and gives it a resource-conservation rationale.
- Poorly: the reading-diet advice is long-horizon and unactionable inside a 4–8 week prep window; no triage for short-timeline students. Publication lists date quickly; extract the PRINCIPLE (active adult-register nonfiction) not the outlets.
- Poorly: no data on family frequency/mix per module — we should compute that from our own bank and official tests rather than trust any book.
