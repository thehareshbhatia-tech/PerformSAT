# Coordinate-plane literacy — reading and naming points

## What it is
The floor under every graph skill: a point is an ordered pair (x, y) — horizontal position first, vertical second — read against two perpendicular number lines that cross at the origin (0, 0). Right and up are positive; left and down are negative. The four quadrants are named counterclockwise from the upper right, and a point's quadrant is fully determined by the sign pair of its coordinates.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
The exam almost never tests plane literacy directly; it taxes it silently inside every graph item. A student who reads (3, −2) as "up 3, left 2," or who swaps the coordinate order when extracting a point from a graph, generates wrong answers that look like slope or function errors when the real fault is one level down. The tutor needs this node to exist so misses can be routed to the true floor.

## Mental model
**Address, then altitude.** The x-coordinate is the street address (how far along), the y-coordinate is the floor (how far up). Every graph operation — reading a value, plotting a solution, checking an intersection — reduces to translating between an ordered pair and a position, in both directions, without hesitation.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Reading a point off a graph: drop a vertical to the x-axis (that number first), then a horizontal to the y-axis (that number second).
2. Plotting: walk x first, then y — always in that order.
3. Sign check by quadrant: (+, +) upper right, (−, +) upper left, (−, −) lower left, (+, −) lower right.
4. Points ON an axis: y = 0 means on the x-axis; x = 0 means on the y-axis — an intercept is just a point with one coordinate forced to zero ([[intercepts-as-anchors]]).

## Misconceptions
- **coordinate-order-swap** — Reading or writing (y, x) instead of (x, y). Forms because "row, column" and "over, up" conventions collide across school subjects; surfaces as consistent reflections of intended points across the diagonal.
- **axis-point-quadrant-guess** — Believing every point lives in a quadrant, so a point on an axis gets forced into one, corrupting sign reasoning. Forms because quadrant drills rarely include boundary points.

## Diagnostic indicators
- Misses cluster on read-the-graph items while equation-only siblings are fine.
- Asked to plot (3, −2), the student lands at (−2, 3) or hesitates about direction.
- Intercept questions answered with the wrong coordinate ("the x-intercept is 4" when the graph crosses at y = 4).

## Remediation pathways
- Rebuild with the address-then-altitude walk on a handful of points across all four quadrants plus both axes; then re-run the missed graph items.
- Pair with [[graph-as-io-pairs]] once reads are clean: the plane is just the function's input-output table drawn.
