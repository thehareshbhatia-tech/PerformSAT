# Number-line order and distance — the two readings of "less than"

## What it is
Two distinct facts a number line encodes, which students routinely blur: ORDER (further left = smaller, so every negative is less than every positive, and −9 < −2 even though 9 "feels bigger") and DISTANCE (how far apart two numbers sit, always non-negative, direction-blind). Keeping order and distance separate is the floor under absolute value, inequalities, and every "between" statement.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
Negative-number comparisons hide inside inequality items, data reads, and solution-set questions. The exam's favorite version: a correct procedure finishing with a wrong comparison (picking −2 < −9 because 2 < 9), which converts a solved item into a missed one at the final step. The distance reading is the direct floor under [[absolute-value-as-distance]].

## Mental model
**Left-right for order, gap for distance.** "a < b" means a sits to the LEFT of b — nothing else. "How far apart" is a separate question whose answer is the gap |a − b|, computed the same whichever side you start from. When a comparison involves negatives, place both numbers mentally on the line and read left-to-right; never compare by digit size.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Any comparison with a negative: place both numbers on the line; leftmost is smaller.
2. Any "distance" or "difference between" phrasing: compute the gap |a − b|; direction and sign come only from the question's explicit ask.
3. "Between a and b" claims: sketch the segment; endpoints included or excluded per the wording.
4. Midpoint of two values = their average — the point equidistant from both.

## Misconceptions
- **magnitude-as-order** — Comparing negatives by digit size (−9 > −2 "because 9 > 2"). Forms because years of positive-only comparison make "bigger digits = bigger number" automatic; surfaces at the final answer step of otherwise-correct work.
- **signed-distance** — Reporting a distance as negative, or believing the answer depends on subtraction order. Forms from conflating "difference" (signed) with "distance" (never signed).

## Diagnostic indicators
- Inequality items solved correctly until the last comparison, then flipped.
- Distance answers that change when the student subtracts in the other order.
- Number-line region questions where the shaded set is mirrored around zero.

## Remediation pathways
- Short placement drills mixing negatives, zero, and fractions; read every comparison as left-vs-right aloud.
- Then re-anchor absolute value as distance ([[absolute-value-as-distance]]) so the two readings stay attached to their questions.
