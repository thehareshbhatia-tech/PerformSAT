# The eight transition categories (and the same-category elimination)

## What it is
Transition questions test ONE relationship: how the sentence with the blank relates to the sentence before it. Students miss them for exactly two reasons — misreading the passage or not knowing the answer categories. Sorting every transition word into eight functional families turns the item into a two-step classification task.

## Mental model
**Name the relationship BEFORE reading the choices, then find the family that matches — and if two choices belong to the same family, both are wrong.** The families: contrast/subversion (however, nonetheless, by contrast), zoom-in/example (for example, specifically), parallel-add (in addition, similarly), cause-effect (thus, therefore, as a result), sequence/time (meanwhile, subsequently), restatement (in other words — usually a distractor), confirmation (indeed, in fact — rare but usually right when present), and frequency/degree (increasingly).

## Expert reasoning process
1. Read the sentence before the blank and the sentence with the blank word-by-word — transition items punish skimming more than any other type.
2. State the relationship in your own words FIRST ("this second sentence pushes against the first" / "this is an example of that") — predicting before peeking blocks distractor implantation.
3. Map your predicted relationship to its family, then scan choices for that family's words.
4. **Same-family elimination**: two choices from one family cancel each other — the test can't have two right answers, so neither is correct. This alone resolves many hard items.
5. Calibration priors: contrast is the most commonly correct family (test writers love turns); restatement is almost always bait; confirmation words are rare but usually correct when offered.

## Misconceptions
- **synonym-blindness** — treating "however" vs "nonetheless" as different options when they're the same functional family; the choice between them is never the real question.
- **first-sentence-only reading** — classifying from the blank's own sentence without the prior sentence; the relationship needs both ends.

## Diagnostic indicators
- Misses concentrated on transition items with two contrast-family choices → hasn't learned same-family elimination.
- Picks restatement words on items whose sentences advance the argument → using word familiarity instead of relationship classification.
