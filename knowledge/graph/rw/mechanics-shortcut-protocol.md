# Conventions shortcuts: choices first, clauses second

## What it is
A workflow for punctuation and verb items that starts from the ANSWER CHOICES — they announce what's being tested — plus high-yield structural shortcuts that resolve most verb items before any deep grammar analysis.

## Mental model
**Look at the choices first: they tell you the game before you read the passage.** All-same-verb choices = a verb item (agreement, tense, or main-verb). Punctuation-only differences = a clause-boundary item, checked in a fixed order: could a colon work (needs a complete clause before it), then period/semicolon/comma+FANBOYS (complete clauses on both sides), then an isolated comma (removable extra info), then no punctuation.

## Expert reasoning process
1. **Classify from the choices** before reading: verb variations → verb item; punctuation variations → boundary item.
2. **Boundary items**: find each clause's subject and main verb, ignoring verbs inside -ing phrases, to-infinitives, and embedded noun clauses. "Comma-jump" over nonessential chunks to see the skeleton. Never punctuate between a title and a name ("architect Maya Lin").
3. **Verb items — structural reads before grammar**: the odd-one-out among choices (three plural, one singular; three -ing forms, one finite) is disproportionately the answer, because the test needs exactly one form that fits. "Will" anywhere in the choices signals a tense item — hunt the sentence's time cues. A word like "by/as/to" right after the blank usually wants the -ed form.
4. **When shortcuts don't settle it**: does the sentence already have a main verb? Then the blank must be a non-finite form (-ing / to-form). No main verb yet? The blank must supply it — then resolve singular/plural or tense from context.
5. **Agreement traps to pre-empt**: "X of Y" agrees with X, not the nearer Y; "X and Y" is plural but "X, along with Y," stays singular; "each" is always singular; watch for subjects that arrive AFTER the verb.
6. **Dashes**: a pair brackets nonessential info; a single dash acts like a colon. Pronoun blanks: match the antecedent's number, and prefer "it" over singular "they" for things on this test; it's/its and they're/their resolve by expanding the contraction aloud.

## Misconceptions
- **read-first reflex** — reading the whole passage before the choices, doing tense analysis on what turns out to be a comma question.
- **proximity agreement** — matching the verb to the nearest noun instead of the grammatical subject; the "X of Y" shape exists to spring this.

## Diagnostic indicators
- Slow-but-right on conventions → workflow gap (reading before classifying); teach choices-first.
- Misses where the chosen verb agrees with the nearest noun → proximity agreement; drill the "X of Y" skeleton read.
