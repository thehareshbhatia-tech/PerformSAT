# Relative pronouns and case: two axes pick the word (who/whom/which/that/where/when)

## What it is
Choosing among who, whom, which, that, whose, where, and when is a single
decision made on two independent axes: (a) what KIND of thing the pronoun refers
to (person / thing / place / time), and (b) what grammatical SLOT it fills in its
clause (subject / object / possessive / adverbial). Every micro-rule in this
family falls out of matching both axes; taught as a rule list it feels arbitrary,
taught as a 2-axis match it becomes mechanical.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
Relative pronouns are where reference meets clause structure, so they probe both
at once. The exam favors the confusions that formality instincts create (whom
"sounds smarter," "where" for abstractions) over rote knowledge. Frequency is
moderate; difficulty is usually medium because the deciding evidence is one word
away from the blank.

## Mental model
**Match type, then slot.**
- Type axis: who/whom = people; which = things; that and whose = either
  (whose looks people-only but is the possessive of both who and which);
  where = physical places ONLY; when = times/events ONLY;
  "preposition + which" is the formally correct substitute for either where or
  when; whereby ≈ "through which," for systems and processes — stilted-sounding
  but legitimate.
- Slot axis (the practical who/whom rule): the SUBJECT form sits before a verb;
  the OBJECT form follows a preposition. A verb immediately after "whom" is the
  error signature. One carve-out: in "quantity + of + whom/which + verb" frames
  ("dozens of whom were…"), the preposition governs the case — the later verb
  belongs to the quantity word, not to the pronoun.
- Redundancy check: once "preposition + which" is used, a duplicate preposition
  at the clause's end is wrong ("in which … in" — one preposition per relation).

## Expert reasoning process
1. Triage: choices rotating through who/whom/which/where/when → this node; look
   at the noun immediately BEFORE the blank (the antecedent) and the word
   immediately AFTER it.
2. Type check: person → who/whom/whose/that; thing → which/that/whose; a
   physical place → where is allowed; a time → when is allowed; anything
   abstract (a book, a theory, a situation) → NOT where/when; expect
   "preposition + which."
3. Slot check for who/whom: next word a verb → who; previous word a preposition →
   whom; apply the quantity-of carve-out before condemning "of whom … were."
4. Scan for duplicated prepositions when "prep + which" appears.
5. Don't eliminate on formality feel — whereby and "in which" are often credited
   precisely because they sound stiff.

## Misconceptions
- **whom-by-formality** — the object form is chosen because it sounds smarter,
  not by structure. Forms because whom survives mostly in formal registers, so
  formality and correctness got fused. The test exploits it by placing whom
  directly before a verb.
- **where-for-abstractions** — using "where" for books, studies, situations, or
  eras. Forms because spatial metaphor pervades speech ("a case where…").
  Produces where-picks whose antecedent is not a physical location.
- **which-for-people** — the person/thing line blurs because colloquial "that"
  freely covers people, and students generalize the blur to which. (The reverse
  usage — "that" for people — the exam simply avoids testing
  [unverified-claim: verify no such items in official digital material].)
- **whose-is-people-only** — students eliminate "whose" with a thing antecedent
  ("a theory whose implications…") because it looks like who's cousin. Forms
  from surface morphology; whose is the shared possessive of who AND which.
- **stilted-must-be-wrong** — whereby, "in which," "of whom" get eliminated for
  sounding awkward. Forms from equating fluency with correctness — the exact
  inverse of the formality error, often in the same student.

## Diagnostic indicators
- Picks whom before a verb → **whom-by-formality**; the giveaway is an
  explanation about tone rather than structure.
- Picks where/when with a non-place/non-time antecedent → 
  **where-for-abstractions**; check whether they can even classify the
  antecedent's type when asked.
- Eliminates whose against a thing antecedent (visible when they choose a wordy
  "of which the" paraphrase instead) → **whose-is-people-only**.
- Eliminates whereby / prep + which choices early and picks a smoother wrong
  choice → **stilted-must-be-wrong**.
- Misses concentrated on "quantity + of whom/which + verb" frames → they know
  the basic slot rule but not the carve-out; teach the exception, don't re-teach
  the rule.

## Remediation pathways
- **whom-by-formality**: replace mnemonics with the two positional checks (next
  word verb? previous word preposition?); drill ten items where whom-before-verb
  is always wrong until the signature is reflexive.
- **where-for-abstractions**: the "can you stand in it?" probe — where is only
  for antecedents you could physically occupy; everything else gets
  "preposition + which." Contrast pair: "the lab where…" vs "the study in
  which…."
- **whose-is-people-only**: show the paraphrase cost — the alternative to
  thing-whose is a clunky "of which" construction the test rarely credits;
  two examples fix it.
- **stilted-must-be-wrong**: reframe — on conventions items, awkward-but-correct
  beats smooth-but-wrong, and the test KNOWS which words you'll flinch at.
  Pair with [[choice-set-diagnosis]]'s style-preference remediation.
- **which-for-people**: quick type-axis sort drill (person/thing/either) on the
  seven words; it's a knowledge gap, not a reasoning gap.

## Mastery criteria
Classifies the antecedent's type and the pronoun's slot out loud, applies the
quantity-of carve-out correctly, and — critically — keeps stiff-but-correct
options alive. Proof: accuracy on items pairing a magnetic smooth distractor
with a stilted credited answer, plus correct where/when rejections for abstract
antecedents.

## Difficulty ladder
- **easy**: person-vs-thing type mismatches (which for a person).
- **medium**: who/whom by position; where/when vs prep + which; thing-whose.
- **hard**: quantity-of-whom frames; duplicated-preposition detection; relative
  pronoun choice embedded in a clause whose placement ALSO matters (composes
  [[modifier-adjacency]] — the clause must touch its noun AND carry the right
  pronoun) or where the relative pronoun's number percolates to a verb inside
  the clause (composes [[subject-verb-agreement-disguises]]).

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: teach only the type axis first (who=people, which=things,
  where=real places, when=times); that alone clears the easy band. Skip whom
  entirely until type is solid.
- **Average**: add the two positional who/whom checks and the where/when →
  prep + which substitution.
- **Advanced**: the carve-outs — quantity-of frames, thing-whose, whereby — and
  the anti-flinch training on stilted forms.
- **1500+**: composition items (relative clause placement + pronoun choice;
  inside-clause agreement) and speed; also make sure over-correction hasn't set
  in (picking whom or whereby BECAUSE it's stiff is the same error mirrored).
