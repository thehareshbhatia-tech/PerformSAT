# Time banking and module position: where writing points live and how to spend the clock

## What it is
The pacing doctrine for the writing side of an R&W module. Writing items, once classified, resolve in seconds; the generous per-question clock exists to subsidize the reading items. Experts answer writing questions fast and reinvest the surplus in reading — and since digital navigation is free, they may deliberately work out of document order. Module ordering is fixed and known (verified against our own bank and the official digital form structure): each R&W module opens vocabulary-first, runs through Craft/Ideas reading items, places **transitions inside the Expression block**, and closes with **notes/rhetorical-synthesis items last** — with at least one notes item effectively guaranteed per module.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
The adaptive engine makes this more than comfort: **module-one accuracy has outsized stakes**, because the second module's difficulty (and the scoring ceiling that comes with it) depends on it. Leaked easy grammar points in module one are the most expensive mistakes on the section. Meanwhile the fixed ordering creates a predictable hazard: the most mechanical, fastest items (notes questions) arrive *last*, exactly when an unpaced student has no clock left — so students who ran out of time forfeit near-guaranteed points that cost under a minute each. The claimed per-question time figure (over a minute per question on the digital format) is **unverified-claim** on the exact number; the strategic asymmetry it implies is what matters.

## Mental model
**The clock is a shared purse, not a per-question allowance.** Writing items are the cheap groceries; reading items are the expensive ones. Every second saved on a classified grammar item is transferred, not discarded. Second invariant: **the module has a map** — you know before you start that the tail is fast, mechanical, and near-guaranteed ([[notes-question-goal-first]]), so the tail must never be starved. Having time is not the same as needing it.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Enter the module knowing the terrain: vocab front, reading middle, Expression block late, notes last.
2. On each writing item, classify → retrieve → check → commit ([[writing-choice-set-classification]]); no lingering after a rule-derived answer.
3. Bank the surplus; spend it on reading items or return passes, not on re-litigating grammar.
4. Protect the tail: reach the closing notes items with enough clock to run the goal-first workflow calmly. Some experts secure the writing tail early (free navigation) and return to flagged reading items after.
5. In module one, apply extra verification on easy items — the marginal seconds are cheap insurance against adaptive downgrade.
6. Treat surface density as a non-signal: length and jargon do not enter the time-allocation decision; rule identity does.

## Misconceptions
- **time-abundance-complacency** — the student reads "over a minute per question" as license to work slowly everywhere. Forms because students don't distinguish *having* time from *needing* it, and pacing advice is usually phrased per-question. Produces starved reading items and abandoned module tails — accuracy on attempted items looks fine, which hides the diagnosis.
- **document-order-lock** — the student processes items strictly in presented order and treats the sequence as mandatory. Forms from paper-test habits and general test obedience. Costs the option value of free navigation: fast guaranteed points at the tail go unbanked when an early reading item eats the clock.
- **uniform-pacing** — the student targets equal time per question as "discipline." Forms from generic test-prep advice. Structurally wrong on a section whose item costs differ by 3-4x; produces rushing on expensive items and dawdling on cheap ones simultaneously.
- **module-one-coasting** — the student treats module one as a warm-up and saves intensity for module two. Forms from sports/game intuitions about pacing effort. Under an adaptive engine this is backwards: early leaked points constrain the entire scoring ceiling.

## Diagnostic indicators
- **time-abundance-complacency**: per-item timing shows writing items consuming near-average time despite high accuracy; unanswered or panic-speed items at the module tail; the student reports "I ran out of time" while their grammar accuracy is strong.
- **document-order-lock**: zero navigation events in telemetry; tail items unreached even when mid-module reading items show extreme dwell times.
- **uniform-pacing**: low variance in per-item times across item types; errors cluster on the expensive reading items that needed the unspent surplus.
- **module-one-coasting**: module-one accuracy below module-two accuracy on comparable items; careless-class errors ([[writing-choice-set-classification]] lapses) concentrated in module one.

## Remediation pathways
- **time-abundance-complacency**: measured proof — time the student on ten classified writing items; the observed seconds-per-item, contrasted with their average, makes the purse model concrete. Then set an explicit writing-item budget and a tail-protection checkpoint ("at question X you should have Y minutes").
- **document-order-lock**: one supervised module where the student secures the closing notes items early via navigation; experiencing the banked points converts better than argument.
- **uniform-pacing**: replace the per-question target with a two-class budget (cheap/expensive) and review timing telemetry together after each practice module.
- **module-one-coasting**: explain the adaptive mechanism explicitly — most students have never been told module one sets the ceiling — and add a module-one verification ritual on easy items.

## Mastery criteria
The student finishes R&W modules with the tail items answered at full quality, shows high time-variance across item types (cheap items fast, expensive items funded), and holds module-one accuracy at or above module-two. Proof is telemetric, not testimonial: no unreached tail items across several practice modules, writing-item median time well under section average with unchanged accuracy.

## Difficulty ladder
This node's "difficulty" scales with stakes rather than item content:
- **easy**: recognizing the module map and answering writing items without lingering.
- **medium**: running a two-class time budget under a real clock; protecting the tail on a hard module where reading items overrun.
- **hard**: full portfolio control — out-of-order navigation, module-one insurance verification, and calm recovery when an early miscalculation has already eaten the surplus. Composes with every item-level node: the cheaper each skill's execution ([[transition-predict-then-match]], [[notes-question-goal-first]]), the more this strategy yields.

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: skip the strategy talk until item-level skills exist — time banking presupposes fast, rule-based writing answers. Teach only "never leave the last questions unanswered; they are the easiest."
- **Average**: introduce the purse model and the module map; practice one module with a mid-module time checkpoint.
- **Advanced**: telemetry-driven review of their own pacing traces; experiment with writing-first navigation and measure whether it helps *them* (it is an option, not a doctrine).
- **1500+**: module-one insurance is the highest-value habit at this level — their errors are rare, so the adaptive cost of each one is maximal; drill fast verification on easy module-one items and tail composure under deliberately engineered time pressure.
