# Supported, not stated: the answer is a paraphrase, the passage holds the proof

## What it is
The information needed to answer a Reading question is always in the passage — but the correct ANSWER is almost never sitting there in the same words. Correct answers restate a passage idea in different, usually more general, language. The act of linking specific passage wording to a re-worded general statement IS the tested skill. Corollary: every single word of a choice must be supported; one unsupported word sinks an otherwise perfect choice.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
The College Board is probing comprehension of MEANING, not string-matching. If correct answers echoed the passage verbatim, the section would reward scanning, not reading. So the test systematically inverts the naive expectation: verbatim-echo choices are frequently traps that recycle vivid passage wording around a distorted relationship, while the credited answer hides its correctness behind synonyms and abstraction. This single design decision generates most of the section's difficulty.

## Mental model
**Passage = evidence locker; answer = testimony.** The testimony must be fully backed by the evidence but will be phrased in the witness's own words. Two graded checks follow:
1. **Same-words suspicion**: a choice that echoes passage wording verbatim deserves suspicion, not comfort — familiarity is the lure, not the proof.
2. **Every word binds**: grade a choice by its weakest word. A choice is a conjunction of claims; each noun's scope, each verb's direction, each modifier's intensity must be individually supported. More clauses = more ways to be wrong.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Locate the passage sentence(s) that bear on the stem (per [[question-first-reading-protocol]]).
2. Restate that content in plain words — this is the "support" you will demand of any choice.
3. Read choices expecting the right one to be a synonym-level restatement, often one notch more general (see [[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]]).
4. For any attractive choice, run the every-word audit: does the passage support this subject at this scope, this verb in this direction, this modifier at this intensity?
5. When a choice reuses exact passage phrases, slow down and check the RELATIONSHIP between the reused pieces — that's where the distortion lives.
6. Presence of a synonym of a passage keyword in a choice is a mildly positive sign; verify by testing one concrete detail of the choice against the referenced lines.

## Misconceptions
- **echo-wording-bias** — "The right answer will use the passage's words." Formed by years of school quizzes where literal word-match earns credit, and by teachers' half-true advice that "the answer is in the passage." Produces picks of verbatim-echo lures and elimination of correct answers for "not sounding like the text." The test writes traps for it deliberately: recycled vivid phrases welded to a twisted relationship.
- **gist-level-verification** — Believing a choice is "supported" because its overall vibe matches, without auditing individual words. Formed because casual reading operates at gist level and usually suffices in life. Produces half-right-hybrid picks (one false element inside a familiar-sounding choice) and intensity-overshoot picks (mild attitude upgraded to extreme).
- **interpretation-earns-credit** — "Deeper reading = better answer." Trained by English-class norms that reward going beyond the text. Produces plausible-but-unsupported picks: choices true in the world, consistent with the passage's vibe, never established by it. The test wants bounded, literal restatement; interpretation beyond the text is a liability.
- **confusion-means-wrong** — "If I don't understand a choice, eliminate it." A comfort heuristic (confusion feels like wrongness). Kills correct answers deliberately written in abstract academic diction — a standard test-writer move that pairs with this exact bias.

## Diagnostic indicators
- **echo-wording-bias**: repeatedly picks the choice sharing the most surface vocabulary with the passage (verbatim-echo species); when explaining, says "the passage literally says this."
- **gist-level-verification**: picks half-right hybrids; explanation defends the true half and never mentions the false element; fast verification times on final choices.
- **interpretation-earns-credit**: picks plausible-but-unsupported choices; explanation cites world knowledge or "what the author probably means" rather than passage lines.
- **confusion-means-wrong**: eliminates the credited answer early when it uses abstract diction; post-hoc says "that one didn't make sense."

## Remediation pathways
- echo-wording-bias: contrast pair — one item where the verbatim choice is a trap and the paraphrase is right, one where the (rare) verbatim-ish choice is right — teaching suspicion, not a ban. If it doesn't land, drill bare phrase-pair matching first ([[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]]).
- gist-level-verification: word-audit ritual — for the final two choices, underline every content word and check each against the text; show them a hybrid choice dissected word by word.
- interpretation-earns-credit: "prove it from the lines" rule — every answer defense must quote or point at specific passage words; revisit [[fiction-poetry-literal-reading]] if the overreach concentrates in literary passages.
- confusion-means-wrong: teach the asymmetric rule — eliminate only on identified wrongness, never on non-comprehension; give them abstract-diction correct answers translated into plain words until the register stops scaring them (prereq support: [[second-sense-vocabulary]]).

## Mastery criteria
Student can take any credited answer and point to the exact passage words supporting EACH content word of it; and can take any near-miss distractor and name the single word or relationship that breaks it. Proof: consistent per-choice justification on medium/hard items, not just correct final picks.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: one-sentence support, one-step synonym swap, distractors clearly off-topic.
- **Medium**: support spans two sentences; correct answer generalizes noticeably; one hybrid distractor.
- **Hard**: correct answer sits at high abstraction in unfamiliar diction while two distractors recycle passage wording; support requires composing [[reference-compression-tracking]] (the key sentence compresses an earlier idea) or spans a qualifier a half-sentence reader clips. At the top end the extremity prior itself gets violated (see [[test-writer-mental-model]]).

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: teach the inversion as a single memorable rule — "same words: suspect; same meaning, different words: promising" — before any nuance. Use short two-choice items (one echo trap, one paraphrase) so the pattern is unmissable.
- **Average**: add the every-word audit on final-two situations only (auditing all four choices is too slow); drill hybrid dissection.
- **Advanced**: their misses are hybrids and scope errors — train weakest-word grading and modifier-intensity checks; introduce the abstract-diction desensitization set.
- **1500+**: teach the exceptions — occasionally a fairly literal choice IS credited, and occasionally strong language is supported. The prior stays, but the passage always overrules the prior; the skill is holding heuristics as priors, not laws ([[test-writer-mental-model]]).
