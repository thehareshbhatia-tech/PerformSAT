# Detail lookup: retrieval plus rephrasing, done with whole sentences

## What it is
The question family phrased "according to the text / based on the text, what is true about X?" or "why (per the text) did X happen?" The idea IS stated in the passage; only the wording moves. These items test retrieval + paraphrase recognition, not inference — which makes them the most protectable points in the section, provided the student reads WHOLE sentences and matches on relationships rather than keywords. unverified-claim: literal-lookup items appear roughly 1–2 per module — a minor but reliable presence; confirm frequency from our bank and official forms.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
It's the floor check: can the student locate stated information and recognize it re-worded? The difficulty, when present, comes from exactly two sources — hard syntax or vocabulary in the PASSAGE, and abstraction in the ANSWERS — never from hidden logic. Knowing this deflates the type: there is nothing to figure out, only something to find and match.

## Mental model
**A matching game between two phrasings of one idea — played in whole sentences.** Two disciplines carry the type:
1. **Whole-sentence reading**: the qualifying half of a sentence (often BEFORE the clause your keyword-scan lands on) frequently contains the answer-deciding fact. Reading from the keyword forward amputates the front half. Always back up to the sentence's start — and often the sentence before, since the entity's introduction may govern everything after ([[reference-compression-tracking]]).
2. **Positive identification over full decoding**: you don't need to parse every archaic word or technical term. If one choice can be positively matched to a stated idea, unknown vocabulary elsewhere — in the passage or in other choices — becomes irrelevant. Find one solid match; stop.

## Expert reasoning process
1. From the stem, name the entity or relationship being asked about — not just the noun, the RELATION ("how A differs from B," not "A").
2. Find where that entity is introduced or actually discussed — not merely where its name last appears; back up to that sentence's start.
3. Translate the relevant text into plain language BEFORE the choices (pre-phrasing immunizes against distractor wording — [[predict-then-verify]]).
4. Match on the relationship, not on shared topic words; expect the credited choice one abstraction rung up ([[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]]).
5. In dense passages (older fiction, science jargon), don't decode everything — hunt the emphasis and causal markers and the sentence boundaries around the reference ([[emphasis-signal-map]]).
6. Once one choice is positively confirmed against the text, commit. Unknown words in the other choices don't need resolving.

## Misconceptions
- **keyword-scan-forward** — Scanning for the stem's keyword and reading only from that word onward. Formed as a speed strategy that pays off on easy items. Fails when the deciding qualifier sits earlier in the sentence or in the preceding sentence; produces confident wrong answers matching the back half, and right-fact-wrong-referent picks when a list or two mechanisms are in play ([[distractor-species-taxonomy]] species 10).
- **echo-wording-bias** — Expecting the answer in the passage's words (see [[supported-not-stated]]); on this type it's especially costly because a verbatim-flavored trap almost always sits beside the paraphrased credited answer.
- **must-decode-everything** — Believing an unknown word blocks the question. Formed by completionist reading habits; produces time loss and panic on fiction/science items where the credited answer was verifiable from the readable parts all along.
- **relation-blindness** — Matching the choice to the passage on topic alone when the stem asked for a specific relation (a difference, a cause, a significance). Produces unasked-comparison picks: textually true, wrong question (species 12). Formed by stem-skimming ([[stem-family-recognition]]).

## Diagnostic indicators
- **keyword-scan-forward**: misses whose credited answer hinges on a front-of-sentence qualifier; telemetry shows landing mid-sentence and reading forward; picks match sentence back-halves.
- **echo-wording-bias**: on this family, near-systematic picks of the maximal-word-overlap choice.
- **must-decode-everything**: dwell spikes on archaic/technical passages ending in guesses; self-report of "couldn't understand the passage" on items answered by plain sentences.
- **relation-blindness**: picks that are verifiably true of the passage but answer a different relation; stem re-read absent in behavior.

## Remediation pathways
- keyword-scan-forward: back-up ritual — on every lookup, the finger/cursor goes to the sentence's FIRST word before reading; contrast pair where the front-half qualifier flips the answer.
- echo-wording-bias: run the same-words-suspect drill from [[supported-not-stated]] scoped to this family.
- must-decode-everything: nonsense-word passages — items remain answerable with two invented terms inserted; teaches positive identification. Longer horizon: register exposure ([[second-sense-vocabulary]]).
- relation-blindness: stem-restatement gate — student states the asked relation in their own words before touching the passage; drill stems that differ only in relation over one passage.

## Mastery criteria
Near-ceiling accuracy on this family at normal pace, with process evidence: backs up to sentence starts, pre-phrases, commits on positive identification without resolving every choice. Proof: this family should be the student's HIGHEST-accuracy reading family; anything else signals process leaks, not knowledge gaps.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: entity discussed in one plain sentence; credited answer a near-synonym restatement.
- **Medium**: qualifier in the front half or previous sentence decides it; passage register rises (science method, older diction); credited answer noticeably more abstract; a right-fact-wrong-referent distractor appears.
- **Hard** (rare for this family): the "stated" idea is distributed across a sentence pair linked by a compression-noun ([[reference-compression-tracking]]), or the passage is late-19th-century fiction where syntax inverts — the ceiling here is passage difficulty, never logical depth.

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: this is the family to build first — wins are cheap and real. Teach only two rules: read the whole sentence from its start; the right answer says the same thing in different words.
- **Average**: add pre-phrasing and the positive-identification stopping rule; kill the keyword-scan habit with contrast pairs.
- **Advanced**: dense-register passages (older fiction, technical abstracts) at full speed; relation-precision on subtle stems.
- **1500+**: these items are pace donors — bank seconds here to fund verification elsewhere ([[writing-module-economics]]). The only remaining risk is complacency: a missed front-half qualifier on autopilot. One deliberate whole-sentence glance is the entire insurance policy.
