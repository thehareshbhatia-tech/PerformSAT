# Context-window discipline — the evidence is rarely where the question points

## What it is
A rule about *where to read*: the sentence containing the blank, target word, or referenced portion only locates the question — the evidence that decides it frequently sits one sentence before or after. The default reading window is the target sentence plus one sentence on each side; if the target sits near the top of the passage, restart from sentence one. A companion structural fact: mid-paragraph material is usually support, and the claim it serves usually lives at the paragraph's start or end — so function evidence tends to point *upward*.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
The digital SAT's short passages create an illusion of locality: the question highlights a specific spot, so students infer the answer must live inside it. Test writers exploit this deliberately — distractors are built from content immediately *after* the referenced portion to punish forward-only reading, and words-in-context items place the decisive contrast one sentence away so that tunnel-vision readers pick the exact opposite of the answer. The underlying ability probed is treating a passage as a connected argument rather than a set of independent sentences.

## Mental model
**The question's pointer is a location, not a boundary.** Picture the referenced lines as a flashlight beam: the beam shows you where to stand, but you must sweep it one sentence up and one sentence down before deciding anything. For "why is this here" questions, sweep up first — support looks upward at the claim it serves.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Read the target sentence for content.
2. Before forming any hypothesis, extend the window: previous sentence, next sentence. If the target is within the first two sentences, read from the top.
3. Ask where the governing material sits: a claim before (most common for function/support), a payoff after (colon setups, question-then-answer structures), or a contrast on either side.
4. Anchor the answer to evidence *inside the widened window* — be able to point at the phrase that forces it.
5. Time discipline: the window is bounded. Widening means ±1–2 sentences, not rereading the whole passage; if ±1 doesn't resolve it, widen once more and stop.

## Misconceptions
- **answer-lives-inside-the-lines** — The student believes the referenced range contains everything needed, because the question "told me where to look." Formed by line-reference mechanics on every prior test they've taken. Produces picks built from the referenced content alone — including the after-the-reference distractor engineered for exactly this reader.
- **blank-line-tunnel-vision** — In words-in-context, reading only the sentence containing the blank. Formed as a time-saving instinct that usually works on easy items. Produces opposite-polarity picks when the decisive contrast lives one sentence away — a distinctive, diagnosable error signature (not just wrong, *anti-correct*).
- **unbounded-widening** — The overcorrection: rereading the entire passage for every question. Formed after being burned by tunnel vision. Produces severe pacing damage and paradoxically worse accuracy (the compact evidence gets diluted by irrelevant text).
- **forward-only-reading** — Widening the window only downward, because reading backwards feels unnatural. Misses the claim-before-support structure that governs most function questions.

## Diagnostic indicators
- **answer-lives-inside-the-lines**: picks the distractor summarizing content just after the referenced portion; explanation cites only referenced-line content.
- **blank-line-tunnel-vision**: picks the polar opposite of the credited answer on WiC items whose contrast is one sentence out; fast answer times on exactly those items.
- **unbounded-widening**: correct but slow; runs out of time in the final third of the module; reports "I reread the passage for every question."
- **forward-only-reading**: accuracy gap between items whose evidence precedes vs. follows the reference; picks after-the-reference distractors.

## Remediation pathways
- **answer-lives-inside-the-lines**: show a contrast pair — the same item answered from the lines alone (wrong) vs. with the prior sentence (right). Name the trap: "they build a wrong answer out of the next sentence for people who only read forward."
- **blank-line-tunnel-vision**: institute the ±1 ritual as a physical habit (finger or cursor moves up one sentence before any choice is read). The anti-correct error signature usually convinces students quickly — show them they picked the *opposite*, not a random miss.
- **unbounded-widening**: cap the window explicitly (±1, widen once to ±2, stop); drill with a timer showing accuracy holds while time drops.
- **forward-only-reading**: teach evidence-points-upward as a structural fact: examples serve claims, claims come first. Drill "find the sentence this detail serves" as a standalone task.

## Mastery criteria
The student widens by default (observable in cursor/scratch behavior), can point at the specific out-of-window phrase that decided each answer, and shows no accuracy gap between evidence-before and evidence-after items — while keeping per-item time flat. The anti-correct (opposite-pick) signature disappears from their WiC record.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: evidence inside the target sentence; window discipline unnecessary (which is what trains the bad habit).
- **Medium**: evidence exactly one sentence away; one after-the-reference distractor present.
- **Hard**: evidence two sentences away or split across both sides; target near passage top requiring restart-from-the-beginning; paragraph structure inverted (claim at the end) to punish rote "look up" application. ## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: one physical rule, no theory: "read one up, one down, every time." Build it as motor habit before explaining why.
- **Average**: add the structural asymmetry (support looks upward) and the near-the-top restart rule; show them a real after-the-reference distractor dissected.
- **Advanced**: train window *calibration* — recognizing which items genuinely need ±2 and which resolve locally, to protect pacing.
- **1500+**: adversarial structure: inverted paragraphs, evidence split across the window, and items where the widened window contains a decoy contrast. Discuss how they'd design such an item.
