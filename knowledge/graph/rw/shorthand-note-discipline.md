# Shorthand notes: scratch work for reading

## What it is
Writing tiny external notes — 2 to 5 words, symbols, arrows, personal abbreviations — at specific moments in the reading process: the passage's point after reading it, both sides of an old/new passage, your predicted answer before the choices, and what you know when stuck between two. Legibility to anyone else is irrelevant. The note is working-memory offload, exactly what scratch work is in math — and answering requires LOOKING BACK at the note, not just having written it.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
Not tested directly — it's the process asymmetry between top scorers and everyone else. Reading items load working memory hard: hold the point, hold the stem's demand, evaluate four multi-clause choices, each trying to overwrite your grip on the passage. Externalizing the point makes it overwrite-proof. On-screen testing raises the stakes: screens invite skipped steps, which strengthens rather than weakens the case for scratch paper. The costliest failure it prevents: polarity flips on belief-revision passages, where a half-remembered map picks the exact opposite of the point ([[belief-revision-template]]).

## Mental model
**The note is a save point.** Distractors are engineered to corrupt your in-head copy of the passage (that's what "plausible" means); a jotted point can't be corrupted. Three-part discipline:
1. **Write small**: a few characters. "old: miasma / new: germs ↑" is a complete note. Anything longer is summarizing, not saving ([[topic-point-attitude-reading]]'s describe-vs-argue applies to notes too).
2. **Write at the trigger moments**: point-after-read (global questions), both-sides (template passages), prediction (hard items — [[predict-then-verify]]), differential (final-two standoffs).
3. **Read it back**: the note only pays when consulted during elimination. Write-only noting is the commonest way this skill silently fails.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Finish the targeted read; jot the point before the choices can speak (≤5 words).
2. On hedge-flagged passages, jot both leaves: "old: X / new: Y."
3. Eliminate WITH the note in view; any choice contradicting the note dies without re-reading the passage.
4. Stuck between two: jot what each claims that the other doesn't, then check that differential in the text.
5. Keep the ritual cheap — seconds, not sentences. If a note takes >8 seconds, it's too long.

## Misconceptions
- **notes-are-for-weak-readers** — Status belief: "I can hold it in my head; notes are training wheels." Held most strongly by fluent readers — the exact population that suffers polarity flips, because their fluency lets them finish passages on a half-encoded map. In reality working-memory offload is a distinguishing habit of top scorers; the time cost is seconds.
- **notes-cost-too-much-time** — Time-pressure logic: "I have 71 seconds; writing is a luxury." Formed by never having measured it. A 4-word jot costs ~5 seconds and routinely saves a 30-second passage re-read during elimination — the note is a pacing INVESTMENT ([[writing-module-economics]]).
- **write-only-noting** — Taking notes dutifully and never consulting them: writing felt like the task, so the loop never closes. Formed because school note-taking is graded on existence, not use. Diagnosable with surgical precision: the student's scratch note is CORRECT and their chosen answer CONTRADICTS it.
- **transcription-noting** — Copying passage phrases instead of compressing to the claim. Formed from school habits of quote-collecting. Produces notes that don't disambiguate choices (a copied phrase matches the verbatim-echo distractor as well as the credited answer) and burns time.

## Diagnostic indicators
- **notes-are-for-weak-readers**: zero scratch/jot activity in telemetry despite polarity-flip and hybrid misses; strong reader profile otherwise (fast, high easy/medium accuracy).
- **notes-cost-too-much-time**: no notes + repeated passage re-reads during elimination (scroll-back events) — paying the cost they think they're avoiding.
- **write-only-noting**: note content correct, answer contradicts it — the single cleanest root-cause signal in this cluster; also: notes present but no gaze/cursor return to them.
- **transcription-noting**: long verbatim notes; note-taking time high; notes fail to prevent echo-species picks.

## Remediation pathways
- notes-are-for-weak-readers: evidence confrontation — show their own polarity-flip miss next to the 4-word note that would have prevented it; prescribe jotting ONLY on template passages initially (minimal ask, maximal payoff).
- notes-cost-too-much-time: measure it live — time their jot (seconds) against their average re-read loop (tens of seconds); the arithmetic is the cure.
- write-only-noting: consult-gate ritual — before selecting an answer, the cursor/eyes must touch the note; in our UI, an on-screen jot field can require a click-to-confirm against the note.
- transcription-noting: enforce the ≤5-word cap and ban quotation — notes must contain a verb of their own; re-drill three-level compression from [[topic-point-attitude-reading]].

## Mastery criteria
On hard and template passages the student jots at the trigger moments, notes are compressed claims (own verb, ≤5 words), and elimination behavior shows consultation. Proof: polarity-flip and final-two-loss rates drop measurably after the habit installs — the note's value is visible in exactly those two statistics.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy** (of the skill, not the item): single-point jot on one-claim passages.
- **Medium**: two-sided jots on belief-revision passages; prediction jots on completion/evidence items.
- **Hard**: differential jots in final-two standoffs; multi-entity ledgers on paired texts (author A vs. author B positions — feeds [[cross-text-relationship-taxonomy]]) and on fiction with several characters ([[fiction-poetry-literal-reading]]).

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: their working memory is the MOST overloaded, so notes help most — but demanding notes on every item overwhelms. One rule: after reading, write the point in 5 words, then look at it while answering. Nothing else.
- **Average**: full trigger-moment system; the consult-gate; timed proof that jotting is cheaper than re-reading.
- **Advanced**: usually the notes-are-beneath-me population. Lead with their own flip data; scope the ask to template passages and final-two standoffs only.
- **1500+**: notes become selective and nearly invisible — a strong 1500+ reader may legitimately skip jotting on most items. The remaining discipline: ALWAYS jot both sides on belief-revision and paired-text items (the two shapes where even elite in-head maps flip), and always jot the differential when a final-two exceeds ~20 seconds.
