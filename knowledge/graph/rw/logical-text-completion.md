# Logical text completion: stating the conclusion the passage already forces

## What it is
A short paragraph builds a line of reasoning and stops mid-sentence at a blank; the task is to choose the statement the paragraph's own logic requires. Despite the "Inferences" label, these are closed-room items ([[premise-completion-vs-new-evidence]]): every premise is on the page, and the required step is the smallest one possible — making the passage's unstated conclusion explicit. The correct answer restates the passage's key idea in *different* vocabulary; it is a paraphrase of what's entailed, almost never an echo of passage wording (unverified-claim on "never verbatim" — verify against our bank).

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
This shape tests whether a student can follow a multi-premise argument to its forced endpoint without adding or losing anything — deductive closure over a paragraph. It appears on every form (unverified-claim on per-form counts) across the full difficulty range, with difficulty controlled by premise density, reference chains, and distractor subtlety rather than by the size of the logical step, which stays small even at the hard end.

## Mental model
**Predict, then shop.** The passage is trustworthy; the choices are adversarial. So derive the completion yourself from the premises — before reading a single choice — then shop for the choice that matches your prediction in meaning (not wording). Two anchors make the prediction fast: the load-bearing sentence usually sits directly before the blank, and any transition word near the blank (*therefore, however, for instance*) dictates the logical direction the completion must take (unverified-claim on "usually directly before" — plausible, verify before the tutor asserts it as a rule).

## Expert reasoning process
1. Read the passage once for its spine ([[argument-spine-decomposition]]), then zero in on the sentence(s) touching the blank.
2. Note the transition governing the blank: consequence markers demand the argument's conclusion; contrast markers demand the opposite pole of the setup; illustration markers demand a specific instance of the idea just stated.
3. Compress the operative claim to shorthand — arrow notation for empirical relations ("shade↑ → yield↓").
4. Chase any pointer phrase ("this possibility," "such a strategy") *backward* until its referent is concrete; hard items make the final sentence unresolvable alone.
5. Apply one or two licensed entailment moves ([[entailment-moves-and-boundaries]]) to produce a predicted completion. If it feels obvious, good — that's the design.
6. Scan choices for the semantic match, verifying it preserves direction, scope, and subject. If the match is overwhelming, commit without a full elimination pass.
7. If no confident match: eliminate by class — contradicts the mechanism; speculation beyond the text; true passage facts recombined into an idle statement; scope shifted; wrong category (list items).
8. Variant — **list completion**: when the blank ends a parallel list, ask "what property do the listed items share?" and require the answer to be another *instance of that property*, not something merely on-theme.

## Misconceptions
- **inference-means-leap** — (shared with [[entailment-moves-and-boundaries]], where it is treated fully) students add ideas instead of closing the given argument, rejecting the "boring" correct completion.
- **echo-seeking** — Students match surface vocabulary instead of meaning: they favor choices that recycle passage words and reject the correct paraphrase. Formed by years of find-the-quote homework where lexical match *was* the skill. Trap design pairs a word-recycling distractor against a synonym-level correct answer.
- **topical-relevance-substitution** — Choices that mention passage entities feel supported regardless of logical function; the full treatment lives in [[topical-vs-logical-relevance]]. On completions it surfaces as picking a vivid detail (a famous name, an associated disease) that the argument never needed.
- **prediction-skipping** — Students read choices first "to save time," letting distractors frame their reading; each choice then gets separately rationalized against the passage. Formed by multiple-choice habits from recognition-based tests. It converts one derivation into four verification jobs and quadruples exposure to traps.
- **transition-blindness** — Students treat the blank as free-standing, ignoring that a *however* or *for instance* just before it constrains the completion's direction; they pick a logically fine continuation of the wrong kind (a conclusion where an example is demanded).

## Diagnostic indicators
- *echo-seeking*: chosen distractors share ≥2 content words with the passage while the missed correct answer shares few; student justifies with "it says that right here."
- *prediction-skipping*: short time-to-first-choice-read, long total time; student cannot state what they expected before reading options.
- *transition-blindness*: errors concentrate on items with a pivot word adjacent to the blank; chosen answer continues the pre-pivot idea.
- *topical-relevance-substitution*: picks the entity-heavy distractor; explanation cites the entity, not the logic.
- *inference-means-leap*: rejects prediction-matching choice as "too simple"; picks speculation distractor.

## Remediation pathways
- *echo-seeking*: contrast pair — two choices for one blank, a word-recycler that's wrong and a paraphrase that's right; have the student translate both into plain meaning before judging. If paraphrase recognition itself is weak, revisit [[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]].
- *prediction-skipping*: cover-the-choices drill (literally cover them) for a full session; the student writes a predicted completion first, every item, and scores prediction quality separately from answer accuracy.
- *transition-blindness*: revisit [[transition-relationship-families]]; then drill blanks preceded by consequence vs. contrast vs. illustration markers, asking only "what KIND of statement must follow?" before any content work.
- *topical-relevance-substitution*: route to [[topical-vs-logical-relevance]] remediation (spine-vs-garnish sorting).
- *inference-means-leap*: route to [[entailment-moves-and-boundaries]] remediation.

## Mastery criteria
The student predicts before reading choices as an automatic habit, and their predictions semantically match the credited answer on a strong majority of medium+ items. They can articulate, per distractor, *which class* of wrong it is. Proof: consistent accuracy on hard completions featuring reference chains and recombined-fact distractors, with prediction-first process visible in their scratch work or think-aloud.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: two or three plain premises; blank follows a consequence marker; distractors are direct contradictions or blatantly off-topic.
- **Medium**: four-plus premises requiring compression; one complement move; speculation and entity-halo distractors appear; vocabulary rises.
- **Hard**: pointer phrases forcing multi-sentence backtracking; double negatives; recombined-fact distractors that sound profound; list-completion category tests; dense diction requiring [[evidence-forced-word-meaning]] decoding before the logic is even visible; hybrids where the premises include a data display ([[claim-anchored-data-reading]]).

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: shrink the task — give them the passage with the choices removed and make "say what comes next in your own words" the entire exercise. Accuracy on real items follows once prediction exists at all. Reassure explicitly that the right answer is allowed to feel obvious.
- **Average**: install the two-step rhythm (predict → shop) and arrow-notation compression. Their misses are mostly echo-seeking and skipped predictions under time; assign written predictions for two weeks, then let the writing fade to mental habit.
- **Advanced**: reference-chain and list-variant work; teach the elimination-by-class taxonomy so their fallback (when prediction fails) is principled rather than vibes.
- **1500+**: hard-band traps only — recombined facts, double negatives, category misses. Have them do post-mortems classifying every distractor on items they got *right*; the goal is making trap recognition explicit enough to survive the worst time pressure.
