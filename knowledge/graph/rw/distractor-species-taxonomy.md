# The distractor bestiary: every wrong answer is wrong for a nameable, checkable reason

## What it is
SAT Reading wrong answers are not random near-misses; they are engineered, and they cluster into a small set of recurring species. Each species exploits one specific student weakness, and each is defeated by one specific check against the passage. Knowing the bestiary turns elimination from vibes into procedure — and turns a student's miss history into a root-cause diagnosis.

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
Distractors are how the test discriminates: a plausible wrong answer must attract exactly the students who lack the target skill and repel those who have it. So each species is a probe. For our engine this is the key diagnostic inversion: WHICH species a student repeatedly falls for identifies WHAT they can't do (scope-inflation picks → ladder problem; echo picks → paraphrase problem; hybrid picks → verification problem).

## Mental model
**Wrong answers are counterfeit bills, and each counterfeit has a signature flaw.** The canonical species:

1. **Off-topic** — about something the referenced lines never address (or a dimension — intelligence, satisfaction — the passage never discusses). Exploits: fuzzy topic grip. Check: does the passage discuss this AT ALL?
2. **Scope inflation** — generalizes a case to a class (one researcher → researchers), pluralizes a singular, or adds a superlative/comparative the text never makes ("most widely read" when the text says "widely read"). Exploits: higher-is-truer bias. Check: match number and breadth against the passage's actual subject.
3. **Extremity** — absolute or limiting language stronger than the passage commits to. A strong PRIOR against, not a law — exceptions rise at the hard end (unverified-claim on frequency; validate against our bank). Check: find the passage words that would license the intensity.
4. **Half-right hybrid** — familiar, correct pieces welded to one false or unsupported element. Exploits: gist-level verification. Check: every-word audit; grade the weakest word.
5. **Plausible-but-unsupported** — could be true in the world, fits the vibe, never established by the passage. The most common finalist-trap (unverified-claim on frequency). Exploits: interpretation habits and world-knowledge verification. Check: point at the supporting lines or discard.
6. **Wrong-span** — true of the passage overall but not of the lines asked about, or vice versa. Exploits: ignoring the stem's scope. Check: re-read the stem's scope words.
7. **Fact-true-text-absent** — factually accurate in reality, absent from the passage. Exploits: verifying against memory of the world instead of the text.
8. **Verbatim-echo lure** — recycles exact passage wording around a distorted relationship. Exploits: echo-wording bias. Check: audit the RELATIONSHIP between the echoed pieces.
9. **Contradiction-in-costume** — states the opposite of the text using enough shared vocabulary to feel familiar (also catches polarity-flips on old/new passages — see [[belief-revision-template]]). Check: direction-of-claim audit.
10. **Right-fact-wrong-referent** — a true detail attached to the wrong subject (the benefit of mechanism A credited to mechanism B; one list item's property generalized to all). Exploits: half-sentence reading and list-blurring. Check: trace the detail back to its owner.
11. **Detail-promotion** — a true, verifiable detail offered as the main idea. Exploits: missing topic→point compression ([[topic-point-attitude-reading]]).
12. **Unsupported-causation / unasked-comparison** — converts co-mention into a because-claim, or answers a different relation than the stem asked. Exploits: relationship-blind matching and stem-skimming.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Predict the answer first ([[predict-then-verify]]) so distractors are compared against YOUR standard, not each other.
2. On each elimination, name the species (silently): "scope," "echo," "not in text." Named kills are confident kills.
3. On the final two, identify which species pairing you're facing — commonly a half-right hybrid vs. a plausible-but-unsupported (unverified-claim) — and run the check that discriminates: verify one concrete detail of each against the lines.
4. In review, log the species of every miss; the histogram IS the study plan.

## Misconceptions
- **wrong-answers-are-random** — "Reading misses are just how I read; the wrong ones were tricky." Formed because Reading feedback feels less rule-governed than math or grammar. Produces no review learning. Reality: nearly every miss has a recurring, nameable cause.
- **plausibility-is-support** — Judging choices by whether they COULD be true rather than whether the text establishes them. Formed by classroom discussion norms where reasonable speculation earns credit. Produces species-5 and species-7 picks.
- **elimination-by-comparison** — Adjudicating choices against each other ("B sounds better than C") instead of against the passage. Formed because comparison feels rigorous. Produces hybrid picks: the choice that SOUNDS most complete often contains the buried false element; more clauses = more failure surface, yet longer choices feel more authoritative (unverified-claim: longer detail-rich choices fail more often — validate before asserting).
- **one-strike-heuristics-as-laws** — Treating "extreme = wrong" or "verbatim = wrong" as automatic kills. Formed from over-taught test tricks. The test occasionally breaks its own patterns, specifically to catch heuristic-only players at the hard end ([[test-writer-mental-model]]).

## Diagnostic indicators
Keyed by picked species (the engine can tag these automatically):
- Repeated species 2/3 picks → abstraction-ladder scope problem ([[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]]).
- Repeated species 4 picks with fast verification times → gist-level verification; verification-skip under time pressure.
- Repeated species 5/7 picks + world-knowledge explanations → interpretation-earns-credit ([[supported-not-stated]]).
- Repeated species 8/9 picks → echo-wording bias / polarity mapping failure ([[belief-revision-template]]).
- Repeated species 10 picks → half-sentence reading ([[detail-lookup-protocol]]) or referent loss ([[reference-compression-tracking]]).
- Repeated species 11 picks → topic-vs-point confusion ([[topic-point-attitude-reading]]).
- Species scattered evenly + chronic final-two losses → comprehension deficit upstream, not elimination technique (see [[predict-then-verify]]).

## Remediation pathways
- wrong-answers-are-random: autopsy ritual — student must name the species of their last 10 misses using the bestiary card; the moment misses become nameable, review starts working.
- plausibility-is-support: "true vs. established" sorting drill — statements about a passage sorted into true-and-supported / true-but-absent / false.
- elimination-by-comparison: enforce predict-first so the standard is external; dissect one hybrid per session word-by-word.
- one-strike-heuristics-as-laws: show a curated set where the extreme or verbatim choice is credited; reframe heuristics as priors that the passage always overrules.

## Mastery criteria
Student can name the species of any distractor on sight with the discriminating check, and their own miss log shows no single species above ~30% of misses (no standing exploit). Proof: per-choice justifications on hard items that name species correctly, sustained across mixed sets.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: one obviously off-topic, one contradiction; species signals are loud (absolute words, alien topics).
- **Medium**: one hybrid + one scope inflation; signals require a targeted passage check.
- **Hard**: finalist pair engineered as hybrid vs. plausible-but-unsupported, both passing gist checks; species cues are quiet (a single verb's direction, a single quantifier); composes with [[abstraction-ladder-paraphrase]] (the credited answer is abstract enough to feel less "supported" than the trap).

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: teach only three species first — off-topic, contradiction, not-in-text — as the "big kills." These alone eliminate 1–2 choices on most items and build confidence.
- **Average**: full bestiary as a reference card; require species-naming in review, not during solving (naming mid-item is too slow at this level).
- **Advanced**: finalist-pair dynamics — drill hybrid-vs-unsupported showdowns with forced detail-verification; introduce their personal species histogram.
- **1500+**: the exceptions become the curriculum — items where the usual prior loses; plus speed: species-naming during solving is now cheap and becomes a live verification layer rather than a review tool.
