# Modeling the test writer: two minds behind every question

## What it is
Every Reading item has two authors: the passage's author and the test's writers. Experts consciously model the SECOND mind — the test writers' notion of what makes an answer correct — and suppress their own opinions entirely. The credited answer has a predictable aesthetic: cautious, passage-bounded, moderate in intensity, paraphrastic in wording. This model also governs the role of outside knowledge (comprehension aid, never decision criterion) and the status of heuristics (priors the passage can overrule, never laws).

## Why it exists / why the SAT tests it
The test is standardized: items are written to a house style, vetted so exactly one choice survives scrutiny, and constrained to be defensible from the text alone. That standardization is exploitable — it's WHY trial-and-error, prediction, and answer-aesthetics all work. The construct probed, indirectly, is intellectual discipline: can the student separate "what I think" and "what I know of the world" from "what this text establishes"? Students who argue with the test are failing that separation.

## Mental model
**You're not answering the question; you're predicting what the answer key says.** Components of the writers' aesthetic:
- **Bounded**: the credited answer never commits beyond the passage. Given a defensible modest reading and a defensible bold one, the modest one is credited.
- **Moderate**: hedged verbs and qualified scope are house style; extremity is usually a distractor's costume — usually. unverified-claim: extreme-language choices are wrong in the large majority of cases, with exception frequency rising at the hard end; quantify from our bank before asserting numbers.
- **Paraphrastic**: correctness hides behind re-wording ([[supported-not-stated]]).
- **Defensible-by-pointing**: for the credited choice there exist specific passage words a rater could point to. If you can't imagine the pointing, you haven't verified.
Outside knowledge: use it to comprehend faster and to kill real-world-absurd choices (legitimate), never to credit a choice the passage doesn't establish. Heuristics ("extreme is wrong," "verbatim is bait"): hold as priors for pre-ranking, and let the passage cast the deciding vote — the test occasionally breaks its own patterns specifically at the top difficulty band, where heuristic-only players get culled.

## Expert reasoning process
1. Pre-rank choices by the aesthetic: moderate, bounded, paraphrastic ones float; extreme, bold, verbatim ones sink.
2. Verify the floaters against the text ([[predict-then-verify]]) — the aesthetic proposes, the passage disposes.
3. When your preferred answer loses, update the model, not the grievance: post-mortem asks "what did I misread or overextend?", never "why did my answer deserve credit?"
4. When the credited answer approaches from an unexpected direction, treat it as data about the writers' angle-of-attack and log it.
5. Deploy outside knowledge asymmetrically: to comprehend and to eliminate absurdity — never to convict or acquit a choice on world-truth.

## Misconceptions
- **arguing-with-the-key** — Treating disagreements with the answer key as the key's errors. Formed by classroom cultures where interpretive debate is legitimate and by ego protection. Produces zero learning per miss — the single most expensive review failure, since every argued-away miss is a lesson refused.
- **world-truth-verification** — Checking choices against memory of reality instead of the passage. Formed because it's how we evaluate statements everywhere else in life. Produces fact-true-text-absent picks ([[distractor-species-taxonomy]] species 7) — and its mirror: eliminating passage-supported answers that feel worldly-wrong.
- **heuristics-as-laws** — Auto-killing every extreme or verbatim-flavored choice. Formed by over-taught test tricks that work at the easy band. The hard band is built to punish exactly this; the student's accuracy inverts on the hardest items and they can't tell why.
- **opinion-leakage** — Letting personal reaction to the passage's subject color choice evaluation (favoring choices that make the passage say something they agree with). Formed because engaged reading naturally generates opinion; the test requires reading AS the author, feeling AS the writers.

## Diagnostic indicators
- **arguing-with-the-key**: review sessions produce justifications of the missed choice rather than diagnoses; repeated identical species of miss over weeks (nothing updates).
- **world-truth-verification**: species-7 picks; explanations cite facts, statistics, or experiences external to the passage.
- **heuristics-as-laws**: strong medium-band accuracy with a hard-band cliff; eliminated-first choices include hard-band credited answers with strong-but-supported language.
- **opinion-leakage**: misses correlate with passage topics the student has stakes in; explanation language is evaluative ("the author is just wrong").

## Remediation pathways
- arguing-with-the-key: install the post-mortem grammar — every miss review must complete "I misread/overextended ___" before moving on; the tutor refuses "the answer was bad" as a terminal state. If resistance persists, show the pointing-test: rater-defensible words exist for the key, none for theirs.
- world-truth-verification: sorting drill from [[distractor-species-taxonomy]] (true-and-supported / true-but-absent / false), plus the asymmetric-use rule stated explicitly.
- heuristics-as-laws: a curated exceptions set — hard official-style items where the extreme choice is credited; re-frame every heuristic in prior language ("usually, so verify — not always, so never auto-kill").
- opinion-leakage: hot-topic passage drills with a stated separation ritual ("the author believes X; I might not; the question is about the author").

## Mastery criteria
The student's review language is diagnostic, not defensive; their pre-ranking matches the credited answer's aesthetic at high rates while their hard-band accuracy shows they verify rather than auto-apply priors. Proof: hard-band items where the usual heuristic fails, answered correctly; miss reviews that end in named process fixes.

## Difficulty ladder
- **Easy**: aesthetic alone nearly solves the item — one modest paraphrase among three loud wrong answers.
- **Medium**: two choices fit the aesthetic; text verification decides.
- **Hard**: the aesthetic misleads — the credited answer is strongly worded (and fully supported) or oddly angled; only students holding heuristics as priors, plus verification, survive. Composes with every content node, since this model is the meta-layer over all of them.

## Teaching notes
- **Struggling**: skip the meta-framing; give one usable rule — "the safe-sounding answer that matches the passage is usually right" — and defer the exceptions until the medium band is stable.
- **Average**: introduce the two-minds frame and the answer aesthetic explicitly; start the post-mortem grammar; asymmetric outside-knowledge rule.
- **Advanced**: exceptions curriculum (heuristic-breaking items); angle-of-attack logging (what direction did the credited answer come from?); opinion-separation on charged passages.
- **1500+**: full calibration — they should be able to WRITE a plausible SAT item for a passage: a credited answer in house style plus four species-correct distractors. Being able to generate the test's mind is the strongest evidence of modeling it; it also makes their remaining misses feel like the writers' rare style deviations, which they log and absorb without ego cost.
