# The Going-Viral Playbook

A synthesis of three operator guides: the organic system behind apps doing
$100K–$400K MRR with zero ad spend, the volume/velocity model behind 5B+
claimed views, and a UGC-at-scale campaign breakdown (100M+ views, $12M+
spent across apps). The thesis of all three is the same: **virality is not
luck — it's a system, and the system has a strict order.** Funnel first,
format research second, warm-up third, volume fourth, infrastructure fifth.
Skip a step and the later steps stop working.

---

## Rule zero: fix the funnel before you chase the spike

The worst possible outcome for an app is going viral before the funnel
works. 50,000 people download, onboarding is clunky, the paywall hits too
early, conversion is 0.3%, you make $800 — and the algorithm moves on. That
moment doesn't come back.

The onboarding architecture that converts:

- **No friction at entry.** Nothing between the user and the product — no
  login or email screen first.
- **8–12 questions that feel personal**, even if the backend doesn't use
  them yet. The act of answering creates investment.
- **An irreversible action before the paywall** — upload a photo, take a
  scan, complete a diagnostic. Sunk effort is the setup.
- **The blurred result.** The user spent 3–4 minutes, answered questions
  about themselves, and can SEE there's a result — just blurred behind the
  paywall. That tension is where conversion happens, not a subscription
  modal on screen 2.
- **Hard paywall beats freemium** — RevenueCat's 2026 report (115K apps,
  $16B revenue): 10.7% vs 2.1% conversion by day 35, a 5x gap. But it only
  earns that when the user already wants the product before hitting it.
  Sequence is everything.
- **A second paywall on dismissal.** Someone who closed the paywall showed
  intent — they're hesitating, not gone. A 40–50% discount framed as
  something they unlocked by saying no catches them at exactly the right
  moment (one app uses a roulette wheel showing 74% off on close).
- **Longer trials win.** Trials of 17–32 days convert 70% better than
  trials of 4 days or less (42.5% vs 25.5%), yet half of all apps use short
  trials. That gap is money on the table every month.
- **The review prompt goes mid-onboarding**, right after a moment of
  delight, before any friction or paywall. One founder got 152 ratings at
  4.8 average from placement alone — the timing, not the product.

Once the funnel converts, marketing compounds. Not before.

## Account warm-up: the invisible variable that caps everything

The most common UGC complaint — "good content, proven format, every video
stuck at 200 views" — is almost always a warm-up failure, not a content
failure. The algorithm decides who sees your content before you post
anything; warm-up is how you tell it who your audience is.

The 3–4 day protocol (before the first post ever goes up):

1. **Scroll your ideal customer's content ecosystem** — seed the FYP with
   specific niche keywords, not random browsing.
2. **Like/save/comment at ~5% rate.** Liking everything flags you as a bot.
   Act like a human, not a task-completer.
3. **Follow 1–2 micro-influencers per day** (5K–150K followers in the
   niche), naturally as they appear — never in bulk.
4. **Repost 1–2 pieces per day**, mixing viral posts with mid-range ones
   (10K–100K views) — this tells the algorithm what content landscape you
   belong to.

The test: after a proper warm-up, the first 5 posts on a fresh account
should all clear 1,000 views. If they don't, audit the process, not the
content.

One rule that gets ignored constantly: **creators on the same campaign must
never interact with each other's accounts.** It collapses their audiences
into one and can flag all of them as coordinated behavior.

## Format research: research first, scale second

The sequence that fails: hire 10 creators, hand them a brief, hope. The
sequence that works:

1. Spend 2–4 weeks studying what ALREADY performs in your niche. Find the
   2M-view videos and study them like film: the hook in the first 2
   seconds, the length, the music, the text overlay, where and when the app
   appears, what the last 3 seconds do to make someone share.
2. **Replicate exactly — not loosely.** Same pacing, same length, same music
   style, same text placement. Virality is engineered from proven patterns,
   not invented.
3. Test 3–5 formats on 2–3 warmed accounts. Find the 1–2 that consistently
   clear 1,000–5,000 views on cold accounts.
4. Scale ONLY those.

One team spent a full month on this before touching volume; when they
scaled to 6 creators, those creators hit 500K views in week one — not
because they were talented, but because the format was proven before it was
distributed. The common mistake is the reverse: 10 creators posting
unproven content at 200 views each, with no way to diagnose whether the
problem is format, warm-up, or brief.

## The integration test

A format fails in exactly two ways: it goes viral but nobody converts (the
product is sitting ON TOP of the content — entertainment, not
distribution), or it converts but nobody watches (the integration is so
heavy it reads as an ad). The game is the balance: content entertaining
enough to go viral on its own, with the product embedded so converting
feels like the natural next move. The app should be the punchline.

**The test: if you can cut the product out of the video and the video still
works, you don't have a campaign — you have a promo.**

## The channels, ranked by what actually happens

**UGC at scale** — the closest thing to a repeatable machine. Creators
build fresh accounts, warm them up, post 30–60 videos/month each. Pay
$10–15 per video base PLUS performance bonuses ($150 at 100K views, $250 at
250K, $600 at 500K, $1,000 at 1M) — the bonus changes the creator's
relationship to the content; they have a reason to care after delivery.
Start with 1–3 creators until the format is proven, then scale to 10–20
running the same formats in parallel.

**Faceless content** — the cheap testing layer. Warning: faceless promo
pages on Instagram that charge low rates are often run from regions whose
audiences don't convert — 500K views, near-zero subscribers. iOS users in
high-income markets convert 3–4x Android globally. Use faceless to test
formats and seed awareness, never as the primary layer.

**Founder-led content** — the best channel with no budget and the most
underrated one with budget. Replicate proven formats with precision. The
hidden payoff: after making 30–60 videos yourself you develop a feel for
hooks you can't articulate — you write better creator briefs, give feedback
that actually improves content, and can kill a weak video before it posts.
Founder-led makes every other channel work better.

**Influencer marketing** — the most obvious method with the worst execution
rate. What works: $1–2 CPM deals capped at $1,000 (the cap protects you
from a $10K invoice on a flop while tying payout to results). Find
non-agency creators by filtering for personal email domains (gmail/outlook
= direct; agency email = 5–10x markup). It's a volume game: ~100 DMs/day,
and your outreach account needs 10K followers or a verified badge for DMs
to even be visible.

**Paid ads** — the most powerful method, but ONLY after organic is proven.
A video that went viral organically has a high probability of performing as
an ad; a video made for ads with no organic validation is a guess that
costs money daily. Start $50–100/day, more creatives in faster, scale
budget +25% per week once CPA is profitable. The strategic role of paid:
it's the floor under swingy organic months.

**Reddit** — the most underestimated channel. Traffic looks small in
dashboards but converts at rates almost nothing touches, because arrivals
are actively searching for a solution. The play: join niche subreddits,
contribute real value for 2–3 weeks, build karma, THEN answer questions
where your app is the honest answer (search "best app for X" — dozens of
threads are waiting). The failure mode: posting a link on day one and
getting banned within 48 hours.

**Equity partnerships** — 20–50% revenue share instead of per-video fees;
the creator becomes a stakeholder who posts consistently as part of their
own narrative. Works only with deep niche alignment — interview multiple
candidates, take calls, check their track record. Cold outreach doesn't
close equity deals; warm relationships do. The wrong partner consumes
equity while harming the brand.

## The content formula behind the views

The content that scales is simpler than it looks, and it works because it
doesn't look like an ad — so the algorithm doesn't suppress it. The
variables that measurably move performance:

- **Before/after contrast** — the higher the contrast, the higher the share
  rate. Exaggerate it, don't be subtle.
- **Hook text placement** — small hooks positioned to the side with
  hyper-specific language beat centered generic ones. "never saw myself
  with the right makeup routine" beats "glow up guide" every time.
- **Caption phrasing** — "i would be unstoppable" beat "the glow up i
  deserve" in testing. These are not equivalent; test both.
- **Posting time** — 6–10pm local time in the target market consistently
  beats morning and afternoon.
- **Trending audio** — the most underweighted variable. The algorithm gives
  trending sounds a larger initial audience. Audio is distribution
  infrastructure, not background noise.
- **Emoji selection and demographic diversity in visuals** — both sound
  trivial, both measurably shift performance by audience segment. Test.

## Volume, velocity, signal density

The claim (single-source, treat the exact thresholds as hypotheses — but
the direction agrees with everything above): the algorithm tiers accounts
by production rate. A few posts a week reads as a casual user, capped at
your warm audience. 5–7+ posts per day reads as a content channel — supply
the platform needs — and gets served cold to find audience clusters.

- **Volume:** 8 posts/day on the channel.
- **Velocity:** all posts clustered inside ONE 2-hour window of peak
  traffic (e.g. 6:00–8:00pm, one post every 15 minutes). Spreading them
  across the day kills the signal.
- **Signal density:** every clip gets a different hook, different opening
  visual, different sound — 8 distinct signals per day for the algorithm to
  test against cold clusters.

Run all three for 14 days (~112 clips) and the claimed curve is: days 1–5
profiling (~168K views), days 6–10 first 1M+ breakouts (~2.2M), days 11–14
winners (25M+), 50M cumulative. Each missing vector cuts distribution by an
order of magnitude. The three failure modes: spreading posts through the
day, reusing the same hook, and — the sneaky one — **stopping after a hit.**
Celebrate a 5M-view clip by resting and the account reweights back to
baseline within 72 hours. Velocity continues through the win.

Claimed tool stack to run it: ~$264/month (Claude for hook variations,
ElevenLabs narration, Arcads avatars, CapCut editing, Metricool clustered
scheduling, TikTok analytics).

## Infrastructure: treat creator onboarding as a product

What separates apps that scale from apps that plateau is not the channel,
the content, or even the format — it's whether creator operations are built
as a system:

- **Creator agreements with confidentiality clauses** before anyone sees
  campaign materials — filters low-effort creators and protects the format
  from competitors.
- **Content banks updated weekly** so creators always have fresh material;
  campaigns compound instead of restarting from zero.
- **Warm-up playbooks that get audited and verified**, not assumed.
- **Verification before payment.** Bot detection plus rule-compliance
  review on every submission. One campaign paid $0 on 44M views that were
  botted or off-format — that discipline is what makes the numbers real
  (152.7M views across 4,048 submissions, every one verified pre-payout).
- **Payout caps change the economics.** A $500–600 cap per creator per
  video (at ~$0.32 CPM, the cap hits around 1.9M views) means every view
  beyond the cap is free.
- **A second incentive layer tied to DOWNLOADS, not views** — leaderboard
  bonuses tracked through link-in-bio drive reach and conversion at once.
- **Live conversion tracking** — match the hour a video goes viral to the
  revenue chart, so you know when a format has peaked in real time instead
  of a week after the budget burned.

The scaling arc that repeats: two months of small $1K–5K format tests, then
first large campaigns, then the spike month (peak days at 28M views), then
sustained 15–22M/day. Momentum only comes from systems built before you
needed them.

## How this maps to SEVA — in order

1. **Funnel first, before any ambassador posts.** Our blurred-result
   moment is the mini-diagnostic: a student invests 24 questions, then sees
   their "why you missed it" diagnosis — that reveal is where the paywall
   tension belongs. And the single biggest test in this doc: our trial is
   3 days; the data says 17–32 day trials convert 70% better. That's a
   one-line change (`TRIAL_DAYS`) worth an A/B the moment billing is live.
   Add the dismiss-triggered second offer (40–50% off) after.
2. **Prove the format before scaling the army.** The ambassador plan calls
   for 20–30 DMs; this playbook says run 1–3 creators first (Iksha + one or
   two ambassadors) until one format consistently clears 1K–5K views on
   cold accounts — then open the floodgates with that format.
3. **Ship the warm-up protocol into the creator content kit**, and add the
   rule to the program: SEVA creators don't follow/like/comment on each
   other's accounts.
4. **@seva.prep is our founder-led channel.** Study what's winning in
   studytok NOW and replicate exactly — before/after score screens and the
   "why you missed it" reveal are our native before/after contrast. Test
   moving posts from 5PM toward the 6–10PM window, and test clustering.
5. **Reddit fits us perfectly and costs nothing** — r/SAT, r/DigitalSAT,
   r/ApplyingToCollege. Two to three weeks of genuinely helpful answers
   from a personal account first; then the app appears only where it's the
   honest answer.
6. **Add the second incentive layer to the ambassador program** when
   formats are proven: small view bonuses (we already structured $25/10K
   for Iksha) and eventually a signup leaderboard — conversion commissions
   alone don't make creators care about reach.
7. **Verify before paying, even at our size.** Eyeball view authenticity
   before releasing payouts; the payout ledger is the start of that system.
8. **Paid ads wait** until we have an organic winner to amplify.
