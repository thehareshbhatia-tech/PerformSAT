### 1.1 Subject-Verb Agreement

**PT4 Q24** (Module 2)
> **Passage:** "In 2016, engineer Vanessa Galvez oversaw the installation of 164 bioswales, vegetated channels designed to absorb and divert stormwater, along the streets of Queens, New York. By reducing the runoff flowing into city sewers, _______"
>
> **Stem:** "Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?"
>
> **Choices:**
> - A) the mitigation of both street flooding and the resulting pollution of nearby waterways has been achieved by bioswales.
> - B) **the bioswales have mitigated both street flooding and the resulting pollution of nearby waterways.** [CORRECT]
> - C) the bioswales' mitigation of both street flooding and the resulting pollution of nearby waterways has been achieved.
> - D) both street flooding and the resulting pollution of nearby waterways have been mitigated by bioswales.

### 5.2 Passage Length and Complexity

**Typical R&W Passage Characteristics:**

| Question Type | Passage Length | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Words in Context | 50–100 words | Single paragraph |
| Main Purpose / Function | 100–200 words | 2–3 sentences to 1–2 paragraphs |
| Text Structure | 200–350 words | 2–4 paragraphs |
| Literary excerpt | 75–150 words | Complete thematic unit (scene or complete poem) |
| Cross-text questions | 100–250 words per text | 2 distinct texts, contrasting views |

### 5.3 Sentence Structure Patterns

**CB favors:**
- Complex sentences with embedded clauses (e.g., "Despite the generalizations... they have used...")
- Appositives for additional information (e.g., "the asteroid 101955 Bennu, a celestial body...")
- Parallel structure (e.g., "...using natural resources more efficiently...")
- Subordinate clauses introduced by "though," "while," "if," "because"

**CB avoids:**
- Fragmented or overly simple sentences
- Colloquialisms outside direct quotation
- First-person opinion without context

### 5.4 Citation and Attribution Patterns

**CB's standard attribution patterns:**

- "According to [Name], a [descriptor]: [claim]"
  - Example: "According to the text, what was surprising to scientists..."
  
- "[Researcher Name] and colleagues [verb]..."
  - Example: "Biologist Valentina Gómez-Bahamón and her team have investigated..."

- "Research conducted by [name] suggests..."
  - Example: "Research conducted by planetary scientist Katarina Miljkovic suggests..."

- "[Name] holds that... [Alternative view]: [new claim]"
  - Example: "Some ethicists hold that... Philosopher L. Sebastian Purcell has shown that..."

### 5.5 Demographic Patterns in Names

**Observed pattern across PT4–11:**
- Diverse international names: Miljkovic, Okolo, Gómez-Bahamón, Tanaka, Garcia, Tan, Wong, El-Khadra, Reyes, Bald, Tsinnajinnie, Herrera
- Avoids stereotyping; names reflect actual researchers
- No tokenization: diversity integrated naturally into content

### 5.6 What CB Explicitly Avoids

1. **Obscure grammar rules:** No tests of "whom vs. who" at high difficulty; no archaic subjunctive
2. **Trick questions:** Correct answer is always defensible; no ambiguous stems
3. **Ambiguous passages:** Every passage has a clear, single intended meaning
4. **Opinion-based literature questions:** Questions ask about textual evidence, not interpretation
5. **Contemporary politics:** No active political debates; historical events are fair game
6. **Gimmicky topics:** No questions about celebrity gossip, viral videos, or trendy slang
7. **Overly clever wordplay:** Transitions and connectors serve function, not style
8. **Multiple valid answers:** Each question has one clearly correct choice

## 6. ANTI-PATTERNS FOR IDENTIFYING INAUTHENTIC SEC QUESTIONS

Inauthentic SEC questions typically exhibit these red flags:

### 6.1 Grammatical Implausibility

**Red flag:** A question where the "wrong" answers are unrealistically incorrect
- Example: A choice with triple negatives or an agreement error so egregious no writer would make it
- **CB Pattern:** Wrong answers contain *plausible* errors that educated writers might make (e.g., confusing similar words, mismatched tense in complex sentences)

### 6.2 Manufactured Errors

**Red flag:** A passage where the grammatical issue feels forced into the sentence
- Example: A passage about bird migration where a pronoun agreement error is wedged in without reason
- **CB Pattern:** Grammatical issues arise naturally from sentence structure, subordination, or coordination

### 6.3 Testing Multiple Skills Simultaneously

**Red flag:** A single question that tests both punctuation AND agreement AND tense
- **CB Pattern:** Each question isolates ONE skill cleanly
  - Example: If testing comma placement, the answer choices differ ONLY in punctuation, not verb tense

### 6.4 Ambiguous or Missing Context

**Red flag:** Unclear what grammatical rule is being tested, or the context is insufficient to determine correctness
- **CB Pattern:** Passage + question always provides enough information to distinguish correct from incorrect

### 6.5 False Dilemmas in Transitions

**Red flag:** Transition options that all seem plausible but the "correct" answer is arbitrary
- **CB Pattern:** CB transitions are semantically distinct
  - "However" = contrast
  - "Therefore" = causation/result
  - "Additionally" = addition
  - Correct choice is unambiguous given passage logic

### 6.6 Apostrophe Misuse as the Only Skill

**Red flag:** Questions testing ONLY apostrophes without embedding in broader writing skill
- **CB Pattern:** Possessive/plural distinction tested as part of real possessive need ("the story's author" vs. "the authors' stories"), not as isolated trick

## 7. QUESTION STEM PATTERNS (Standard Phrasings)

### 7.1 Standard English Conventions Stem

> "Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?"

This stem appears in **~60% of Module 1–2 mixed questions** and tests:
- Subject-verb agreement
- Pronoun agreement
- Verb tense/form
- Punctuation
- Sentence boundaries
- Modifier placement
- Parallel structure

### 7.2 Words in Context Stem

> "Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?"

Tests vocabulary in context. Always includes 4 words/phrases, each plausible in isolation but only one correct in passage context.

### 7.3 Main Purpose / Function Stem

> "Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?"
> "Which choice best describes the function of [underlined portion] in the text as a whole?"

Tests comprehension of passage macrostructure or sentence function.

### 7.4 Transition Stem

> "Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?"

Tests coherence and logical flow. Transition options are always semantically distinct.

### 7.5 Inference / Logic Stem

> "Which choice most logically completes the text?"
> "Based on the text, how would [author] most likely respond to...?"

Tests inference from textual evidence.

### 7.6 Evidence / Data Stem

> "Which choice most effectively uses data from the [table/graph] to complete the statement?"
> "Which quotation from [work] most effectively illustrates the claim?"

Tests ability to select supporting evidence from data or literature.

### 7.7 Rhetorical Synthesis Stem

> "The student wants to [accomplish X]. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?"

Tests synthesis of multiple pieces of information toward a specific rhetorical goal.

## Summary: Checklist for Authentic CB Questions

✓ **Standard English Conventions:**
- Tests ONE skill cleanly (not multiple simultaneously)
- Correct answer contains a defensible grammatical rule
- Wrong answers are plausible errors (not egregiously wrong)
- Passage context makes correctness determinable

✓ **Words in Context:**
- Four semantically distinct choices
- Only one fits passage meaning
- Vocabulary is college-level or above, not overly obscure

✓ **Reading Comprehension (Main Purpose, Function, Structure):**
- Based on textual evidence, not inference beyond text
- All choices are thematically plausible
- Correct choice is objectively defensible from passage

✓ **Transitions:**
- Choices are semantically distinct (not near-synonyms)
- Correct choice creates logical flow
- Passage logic determines answer, not stylistic preference

✓ **Rhetorical Synthesis:**
- Goal is clearly stated
- Notes contain sufficient information
- Correct answer directly serves stated goal
- Distractors fail the goal or contain extraneous info

✓ **Overall Authenticity:**
- Passage topics: diverse (science, literature, history, culture)
- Names: realistic and diverse
- Passage style: formal, neutral, exposition-based
- No partisan opinion, gimmicks, or trick questions
- Grammar/punctuation rules tested are subset CB has consistently asked across all 8 tests
